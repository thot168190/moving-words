S1-088 | Cinematic progressive pencil-and-ink line animation on a solid pure bright white background, edge to edge. The very first frame is an entirely empty white field. The illustration draws itself - no artist, no hand, no pen or tool ever appears in the frame at any moment. For the opening moment the field stays almost completely empty - the very first pencil stroke appears alone, clearly visible, before any other line follows; the joy of the film is watching the drawing happen. Static locked-off camera, one continuous 8-second take. The subjects: a station clock with a moving second gear beside a departure mechanism held at pause, one train waiting at the platform while another line continues in constant motion, a delayed signal weight hanging above its due release point, a platform canopy cropped by the top edge, and parallel rails extending toward an apparently endless horizon. There are no people, no human figures, anywhere in the scene. 0-3.5s: the picture draws itself stroke by stroke - at first slowly, each graphite line GROWING from one end to the other with its moving tip clearly visible, one line at a time; then the pace quickens like a hand-drawn time-lapse until the sketch is complete. The lines are light yet crisp, clearly legible as they appear, each drawn once. Nothing is revealed by a sweeping wipe and nothing fades into view - every line extends from its own tip. No dense cross-hatching, no engraving-style shading; only airy contour lines. The large landscape forms are drawn first with economical, selective detail, the main subject last, in a few precise strokes. The whole drawing gathers compactly toward the center of the frame, occupying roughly the middle two-thirds, with clean white margins on every side - those margins are reserved for word labels added later, so no element is ever stretched outward to fill the wide frame. The composition is gently asymmetric. Built progressively, never fading in. 3.5-5.5s: pale watercolor tints rise FROM INSIDE the pencil lines, within the lines — a restrained palette of navy on the waiting train, warm brass on the clock and mechanism, restrained coral on the held signal, and pale turquoise along the distant rails, each heavily diluted with water. The larger the area, the lighter and more watery its wash — broad fields, hills and skies receive only the palest, water-flooded tint, far lighter than the small subjects. Color blooms ONLY onto the drawn subjects — all surrounding space stays pure untouched white. The delicate pencil drawing remains the protagonist; color is only a light stain resting on the lines. Generous areas of the paper stay untouched white, and the painting dissolves into untouched white paper well before the frame — soft, wet watercolor boundaries, with generous white margins breathing on at least three sides of the image. One consistent light direction with soft believable shadows. The white background stays pure white. The sky, when present, stays essentially untouched white paper — only the palest breath of tint near the horizon and a few light pencil cloud outlines, never a fully painted sky. 5.5-8s: the final gear completes one minute, the held weight drops in an instant, and the signal changes immediately; the waiting train begins moving after the long pause. Everything else stays perfectly still.
