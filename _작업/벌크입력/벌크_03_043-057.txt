The subjects: two viewers regarding the same abstract sculpture from opposite sides, one seeing a bird form and the other imagining a sailing form, a familiar shell beside the base prompting recognition, a tilted frame cropped in the foreground, and an immense curved gallery wall bending both sightlines toward the object. 0-3.5s: delicate light-graphite pencil strokes are traced visibly from endpoint to endpoint — soft, feather-light, barely pressed, like an artist's first gentle sketch, each line drawn once. No dense cross-hatching, no engraving-style shading; only airy contour lines. The large landscape forms are drawn first with economical, selective detail - a few confident lines suggest each mass, and at least half of every form is left as bare white paper, detail reserved for the focal point only, the main subject last, in a few precise strokes. The composition is asymmetric — the main subject sits off-center. Built progressively, never fading in. 3.5-5.5s: pale watercolor tints rise FROM INSIDE the pencil lines, within the lines — a restrained palette of teal and warm coral on opposite faces of the sculpture, pearl ivory on the shell, ochre yellow on the gallery floor, and pale cobalt along the curved wall, each heavily diluted with water. The larger the area, the lighter and more watery its wash — broad fields, hills and skies receive only the palest, water-flooded tint, far lighter than the small subjects. Color blooms ONLY onto the drawn subjects — all surrounding space stays pure untouched white. The delicate pencil drawing remains the protagonist; color is only a light stain resting on the lines. Generous areas of the paper stay untouched white, and the painting dissolves into untouched white paper well before the frame — soft, wet watercolor boundaries, with generous white margins breathing on at least three sides of the image. One consistent light direction with soft believable shadows. The white background stays pure white. The sky, when present, stays essentially untouched white paper — only the palest breath of tint near the horizon and a few light pencil cloud outlines, never a fully painted sky. 5.5-8s: each viewer shifts one step and the sculpture's two forms briefly overlap; the shell catches light and both glance down, reminded that perspective shapes what they see. Everything else stays perfectly still. Style: delicate pencil-and-ink drawing with airy line weight and sparse, selective detail - generous untouched paper inside the forms, fresh luminous watercolor, clear summer daylight, cinematic depth of foreground, middle ground and far horizon — the feel of a beautiful illustrated atlas for curious teenagers, never a kindergarten picture book. Museum-quality contemporary illustration.
