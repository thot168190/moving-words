The illustration draws itself - no artist, no hand, no pen or tool ever appears in the frame at any moment. Static locked-off camera, one continuous 8-second take. The subjects: one experienced guide showing one learner how to measure river depth with a marked pole, a survey rope stretched between two banks, sample jars arranged by distance, a field case cropped by the lower edge, and a broad river valley placing the small technical exercise inside its real subject. 0-3.5s: delicate light-graphite pencil strokes are traced visibly from endpoint to endpoint — soft, feather-light, barely pressed, like an artist's first gentle sketch, each line drawn once. No dense cross-hatching, no engraving-style shading; only airy contour lines. The large landscape forms are drawn first with economical, selective detail - a few confident lines suggest each mass, and at least half of every form is left as bare white paper, detail reserved for the focal point only, the main subject last, in a few precise strokes. The composition is asymmetric — the main subject sits off-center. Built progressively, never fading in. 3.5-5.5s: pale watercolor tints rise FROM INSIDE the pencil lines, within the lines — a restrained palette of clear turquoise across the river, olive green on the banks, warm saffron on the field case and pole, and indigo on the guide's jacket, each heavily diluted with water. The larger the area, the lighter and more watery its wash — broad fields, hills and skies receive only the palest, water-flooded tint, far lighter than the small subjects. Color blooms ONLY onto the drawn subjects — all surrounding space stays pure untouched white. The delicate pencil drawing remains the protagonist; color is only a light stain resting on the lines. Generous areas of the paper stay untouched white, and the painting dissolves into untouched white paper well before the frame — soft, wet watercolor boundaries, with generous white margins breathing on at least three sides of the image. One consistent light direction with soft believable shadows. The white background stays pure white. The sky, when present, stays essentially untouched white paper — only the palest breath of tint near the horizon and a few light pencil cloud outlines, never a fully painted sky. 5.5-8s: the learner lowers the pole until it touches the riverbed and ties the rope at the matching point; the guide nods as a bright current line passes the correctly placed sample jar.
